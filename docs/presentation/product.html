Who do I field this round?

What you can ask

Four questions, in your own words

X or Y?

“Beto o Laurienté?”

The most common question, often minutes before the deadline. You get the verdict first, with bench advice when it matters: “start X, put Y first on the bench”.

My eleven

“Here's my squad. Who plays this round?”

The eleven, the bench in order for each role, the formation, the expected score and the chance of reaching each of your league's goal thresholds.

Formation

“Fourth defender or fourth midfielder?”

It compares the players on the edge of the team, so the formation follows from who is worth fielding.

The fixture

“What's the story for Genoa–Lazio?”

Form, goals for and against, injuries, suspensions, penalty takers and midweek European games, plus what each means for the players you own.

It plays by your league's rules: bonuses, maluses, modifiers, bench size and substitutions. Tell it once and it remembers. If you don't, it uses standard Classic rules and says so.

How it decides

The rating and the bonuses are priced apart

Voto6.5
Goal+3
Assist+1
Yellow−0.5
Fantavoto10

Averaging a striker's fantavoti blurs his goals into his ratings. Fantadamus estimates the rating and the chance of each bonus separately, then adds them up under your league's rules.

Bonuses come from the market

How many goals a team should score comes from the betting odds for the match. A player's chance of scoring is his share of those goals.

Five games prove little

Four goals in five matches is mostly luck until more games back it up. Every form figure is pulled toward the usual level for the role.

A doubt can cost nothing

If your bench covers him, a no-show costs nothing. A late cameo does cost, because his rating blocks the substitute.

Disagreement stays visible

Probable lineups come from two or three outlets. When they disagree, the answer says so instead of averaging it away.

News moves certainty

A new coach or a rumour mostly makes it less clear who plays. Midweek European games are the exception: they really do bring rotation.

It keeps score of itself

Every probability it gives is logged and checked after the round, and its starting assumptions are recalibrated from what actually happened.

What happens when you ask

Your Claude reads, Fantadamus decides

  1. You ask in your Claude app

    “Beto o Laurienté?”

  2. If anything is stale, your Claude fetches it

    Fantadamus says which facts are missing and which approved sites have them. Your Claude reads those pages and hands over what they say, with the link and the time.

    refreshing Genoa's lineup news…

  3. Fantadamus checks the facts and does the maths

    Each fact is checked for its format, its source and the player's name. All the arithmetic is Fantadamus's own tested code. Your Claude never works out a probability.

  4. You get the answer

    The verdict, the margin and the reasons, each traceable to its source. Facts one of you collects serve the other too.

The small print

What it costs, and what stays private

Cost

  • Nothing to subscribe to. It runs inside your Claude app, so collecting uses your Claude plan's allowance.
  • Hosting costs cents. At two players' volume the AWS bill is expected to stay in cents a month, with a €20 budget alarm to catch mistakes.
  • No data feeds to buy. It reads public pages from an approved list.

Privacy and fair use

  • Your squad and rules stay private. They never go into the public code.
  • Approved sites only. It never signs in, gets round a paywall or reads YouTube.
  • Facts, not pages. It keeps what a page says and its link, never the page itself.
  • Pundits stay anonymous. Their picks show up as “pundit consensus”.

Trades, auctions and market values are out of scope.
